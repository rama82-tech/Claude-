# How to Use Solar Panels: Training Guide

Companion to `solar_training.mp4`, where the BL Agent demonstrates each step.
This is general guidance for small off-grid or hybrid systems. Always follow your local electrical code and the panel and controller manuals. Grid-tied systems need a licensed electrician and utility approval.

## 1. Safety first
- Solar panels produce voltage whenever light hits them, even if "switched off". Keep them covered with an opaque sheet while wiring.
- Wear a hard hat, safety glasses and insulated gloves. Remove metal jewelry and keep your hands dry.
- Open the DC isolator or breaker before you touch any wiring.
- Secure the ladder and never work on a roof alone.
- Never touch bare connectors. Treat every wire as live until you have measured it.

## 2. Choose the site
- Face the panels toward the sun: south in the northern hemisphere, north in the southern.
- Start with a tilt close to your latitude, then adjust for the season if you want to.
- Avoid shade between about 9am and 3pm. Even a small shadow on one cell can cut output sharply.
- Check that the roof or ground frame can carry the weight and the wind load.

## 3. Mount the panel
1. Fix the rails to the rafters or the ground frame.
2. Clamp the panel to the rails with the supplied clamps and torque them to the manufacturer's spec.
3. Leave an air gap under the panel so it stays cool and keeps its output.
4. Keep the panel covered until the wiring is finished.

## 4. Wire the system
Typical off-grid layout: **panels → charge controller → battery → inverter → loads**.

| Order | Connection | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Battery → charge controller | Connect first, so the controller can detect the battery voltage. Fit a fuse or breaker near the battery. |
| 2 | Panels → charge controller | Do this with the panels covered. Use MC4 connectors, correct polarity and a DC isolator. |
| 3 | Inverter and loads | Connect the inverter to the battery through its own fuse. Switch on the loads last. |

- Size the cable for the current and the run length, and keep voltage drop low.
- Put overcurrent protection (fuse or breaker) on every source.
- To shut the system down, **reverse the order**: loads, then inverter, then panels, then battery.
- Check the panel string voltage (Voc) against the controller's maximum input voltage, especially in cold weather.

## 5. Switch on and operate
1. Remove the cover and close the breaker.
2. Check that the controller shows "charging".
3. Check that the voltage and current are in the expected range.
4. Switch on the inverter, then the loads.
5. Watch the battery state of charge and avoid deep discharge.

## 6. Maintenance
- Clean gently with water and a soft cloth, at dawn or dusk when the glass is cool.
- Never use a pressure washer, abrasive pads or strong chemicals.
- Check cables, connectors and clamps monthly for looseness, heat marks or animal damage.
- Look for new shade from trees or buildings, cracked glass or hot spots.
- Log the output every month. A steady drop means it is time to clean or inspect.
- Do not clean or touch a cracked or damaged panel. Isolate it and call a professional.

## Quick quiz
1. Why cover the panels while you wire them?
2. Which do you connect first, battery or panels?
3. What order do you use to shut the system down?
4. When is the best time to clean the panels, and with what?
5. When must you call a licensed electrician?

**Answers:** 1. They make voltage in any light. 2. The battery. 3. The reverse of the connection order. 4. Dawn or dusk, with water and a soft cloth. 5. For grid-tied systems, or whenever you are unsure.
