ATTACK 1 [FATAL] Stage 3 closes door gaps, so Stage 4 has nothing to detect and rooms cannot be built
Where: Stage 3 "close small gaps (door gaps are not wall gaps; see stage 4)" and gate "every wall endpoint must meet another wall"; Stage 4 "Doors: a gap in a wall plus an arc... Rooms: polygonize the closed wall network"
Problem: The two stages need opposite wall data and the plan never says how they connect. Door and window detection depends on the gap in the wall. Shapely polygonize needs a closed ring with no gaps. If Stage 3 closes the gaps, the openings are gone. If it keeps them, every door leaves the wall network open and polygonize returns no closed room, or merges rooms through the gap. Counterexample: a rectangle split into two rooms by a partition with a 0.9 m door. Keep the gap and the partition's walls are two free-ended segments, so the two rooms are not separated. Close it and no door is found. The step that turns gap-split segments into continuous walls plus an opening record is missing. That step is the core of the pipeline. The user cannot implement it without inventing it.

ATTACK 2 [MAJOR] The scale gate contradicts Assumption 2 and the user-override path
Where: Assumption 2 "at least one printed dimension, or a known door/scale bar"; Stage 2 order of trust "(a) a user-supplied distance"; Stage 2 gate "at least two independent dimensions agree within 3 percent. Otherwise stop"; risk table "refuse single-source scale"
Problem: A plan with exactly one printed dimension, or one user-supplied distance, is explicitly allowed by Assumption 2 and ranked first in trust. The gate then rejects it, because it needs two. The step-0 "ask for one real distance" fallback also cannot pass its own gate. The v1 minimal path ("scale taken from one known measured length") bypasses the gate, so the gate is never specified for the case the user is most likely to have. The 3% tolerance also conflicts with Stage 6's 5% disagreement flag. Rounded printed dimensions on real plans routinely differ by more than 3%, so the gate would stop valid plans.

ATTACK 3 [MAJOR] The v1 success test is not valid for the geometry the plan itself defines
Where: "Success test for v1: the exported model's room areas match the plan's printed areas within 3 percent"; plan.json walls use centreline "a","b" plus "thickness"; Stage 7 "assert the bounding box matches the plan within 1 cm"
Problem: Printed room areas on plans are usually net interior areas. The rooms polygon is derived from the wall network, and the plan does not say whether that is centreline or interior. Counterexample: a 4.0 m by 4.0 m clear room with 0.2 m walls has a centreline polygon of 4.2 m by 4.2 m, which is 16.8% over. A correct model fails the 3% test. Likewise, a bounding box built from wall endpoints is 0.1 m smaller on each side than the extruded geometry, so the 1 cm assertion fails on every correct model. Printed areas often do not exist on a hand-sketched plan either. The plan gives no definition that makes these checks pass for a correct build.

ATTACK 4 [MAJOR] Minimal first version does not turn any floor plan into 3D
Where: "Minimal first version" step 1 "Skip CV at first. Hand-write plan.json..." and step 5 putting every image/PDF step after v1
Problem: The task asks for a minimal first version of turning a 2D floor plan (an image, scan or PDF) into a 3D model. The v1 takes no image, PDF or OCR input. The user hand-transcribes the walls. The cheapest way to get image to plan.json is the plan's own "optional Claude vision" proposal, which the user already has in a Claude Code session. That is deferred to the last item (f). So after a weekend the user has a JSON-to-GLB tool, not the stated idea. The plan also never gives a v1 route for a plan with a reasonably clean PDF, even though it describes PyMuPDF vector extraction as the high-confidence route.

ATTACK 5 [MAJOR] Validation checks produce false errors on normal wall geometry, and the checks are blocking
Where: Stage 6 "no overlapping walls... any error-level check blocks the build"; Stage 4 gate "each opening lies fully inside one wall's length"; Stage 4 gate "each room has an area between 1 and 150 m2"
Problem: Walls are stored as thick centreline segments. At every corner and T-junction, two such walls overlap by their thickness, so "no overlapping walls" reports an error on any real plan unless an exemption is defined, and none is given. An opening near a corner, such as a door 0.1 m from a corner, spans the junction and fails "fully inside one wall's length". A 0.8 m2 pantry, closet or shaft fails the 1 m2 room minimum, and the gate is error-level, so it blocks the entire build. The user cannot tell which of these are intended.

ATTACK 6 [MAJOR] Environment fallback is stated but not buildable as written
Where: Assumption 6 "Blender can be installed with pip (bpy) or the Linux tarball. This must be verified in step 0"; "Fallback 3D (no Blender)... trimesh + manifold3d"
Problem: The only v1 deliverable (build.py) is written for `blender -b -P`, and the fallback has no spec: no mapping from plan.json to trimesh, no statement of which outputs it can produce. Cycles materials, Nishita sky and boolean "exact solver" all disappear on that path. The pip bpy wheel is tied to one specific Python minor version and is hundreds of MB, and the container network is behind a proxy, so the install may not work. The plan gives no pinned Blender or bpy version, so the "Blender 4.x/5.x" API differences (for example EEVEE engine names and glTF export options) are left to the user.

ATTACK 7 [MINOR] Camera and deskew rules fail on common inputs
Where: Stage 8 "eye-level camera at 1.6 m in each room's centroid"; Stage 1 "reject if the skew exceeds about 10 degrees (probably a phone photo...)"
Problem: The centroid of an L-shaped or U-shaped room lies outside the room, so the camera sits inside a wall or in the neighbouring room. Use a point guaranteed inside the polygon, such as the pole of inaccessibility. Separately, a flat scan rotated by 15 degrees, or a plan with legitimately diagonal walls that skews the dominant Hough angle, is rejected as a "phone photo" even though no perspective distortion is present. A rotation of 90 degrees is not mentioned either.
