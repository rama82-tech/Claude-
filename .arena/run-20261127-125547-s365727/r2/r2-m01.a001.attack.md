ATTACK 1 [MAJOR] Fast path has Claude read coordinates, which the plan itself says Claude cannot do
Where: Tools table, "Vision LLM ... Poor at pixel coordinates, so never use it to measure." versus Minimal first version, "A faster path that is useful in about 4 hours: ... the walls and openings written by Claude from the image into `plan.json`", and Step 2, "The user, or Claude reading the image, picks two pixel points".
Problem: The only path offered that works without the 3 to 5 hour detector depends on Claude estimating wall endpoints and scale points from an image. The document forbids exactly that and provides no grid overlay, anchor helper or coordinate-reading tool to make it workable. A user taking the 4-hour path has no defined way to get coordinates, so they must guess. The "about 4 hours" figure also leaves out the hours for the generator, viewer and scale steps it lists. Those steps (1 to 4 and 6) sum to roughly 4.4 to 5.6 h by the document's own estimates.

ATTACK 2 [MAJOR] Vendoring three.js as described produces a viewer that does not load
Where: Step 7, "`npm pack three`, unpack, copy `build/three.module.js`, `examples/jsm/loaders/GLTFLoader.js`, `examples/jsm/controls/OrbitControls.js` and its imports into `web/vendor/`", with import map `"three": "./vendor/three.module.js"`.
Problem: No three.js version is pinned. `npm pack three` fetches the latest release. Since r171, `build/three.module.js` imports `./three.core.js`, so copying only `three.module.js` gives a 404 and the page never sets `window.ready`. `GLTFLoader.js` imports `../utils/BufferGeometryUtils.js`. Copying it flat into `web/vendor/` makes that resolve to `web/utils/...`, which does not exist. The viewer, and so every screenshot (`top`, `iso`, `eye`), fails. The 60 s timeout only surfaces the failure after the fact.

ATTACK 3 [MAJOR] "Union ... so corners ... are clean" is false for flat-capped centreline walls
Where: Step 5.1 to 5.2, "`LineString.buffer(t/2, cap_style='flat')` (cap_style=2). Union all walls into one polygon so corners and T-junctions are clean", plus Getting each element right, "Snap endpoints to a 5 cm grid".
Problem: With flat caps, walls that meet at an L corner at centreline point (0,0) cover x in [0,8], y in [-t/2,t/2] and x in [-t/2,t/2], y in [0,5]. The square x in [-t/2,0], y in [-t/2,0] is covered by neither. With a 0.23 m exterior wall, every outer corner of the building has a missing 11.5 cm by 11.5 cm notch in the extruded walls. Snapping endpoints to a grid does not fix this. The doc never mentions extending wall ends by t/2 or using a mitred join, yet its accuracy checks assume a closed, correct footprint.

ATTACK 4 [MAJOR] Accuracy check 2 compares printed dimensions to centreline length, contradicting check 1 and flagging correct plans
Where: Accuracy checks, "2. Each OCR'd or manually measured dimension versus the matching wall length, within 2 percent." Check 1 says "centreline math is never used."
Problem: Printed dimensions are inside-face (room sizes) or outside-face (overall sizes). `plan.json` walls are centrelines. A correct 3.00 m inside-width room between 0.23 m walls has a centreline spacing of 3.23 m, which is 7.7% off and fails. A 10.00 m overall dimension (outside-face) against a 9.77 m centreline wall is 2.3% off and fails. The plan stores no per-dimension basis (inside, outside or centre) and no wall-thickness correction. Correct drawings therefore produce false failures, or the user is pushed to move correct walls.

ATTACK 5 [MAJOR] Wall-style detection and the raster Style B mask depend on a wall thickness that has not been measured yet
Where: Step 3, "classify the wall style ... fraction of dark pixels in the area between candidate parallel lines"; Raster path Style B, "morphological closing with a kernel about the wall thickness"; and "estimate thickness from the distance transform on the skeleton" (the last item of the raster path).
Problem: This is circular. Classification needs candidate parallel lines, which need walls. The closing kernel needs thickness, which is only estimated after the mask and skeleton exist. No method gives the starting thickness for a raster scan. A kernel set to the real gap does not close a gap equal to it (closing fills gaps strictly smaller than the kernel). A kernel that is too large also closes door openings and thin rooms such as a 0.9 m closet, and the doc's only guard is a vague "area much greater than perimeter times thickness". Plans often mix styles (solid exterior, double-line interior), but the doc picks one style per plan, so on such a plan one set of walls is missed or phantom. "If the style is unclear, ask or show both overlays" does not say what to do on a mixed plan.

ATTACK 6 [MAJOR] Opening placement and validation can notch adjoining walls, and nothing prevents it
Where: Step 5.5, "make a box through the wall thickness at `offset`, `width`, `height` and `sill`, and subtract it", and Accuracy check 4, "at least 10 cm of wall on each side". Walls are unioned first (5.2) and `offset` is measured along the centreline from `a`.
Problem: At a corner or T-junction the centreline endpoint lies inside the perpendicular wall's thickness. With 0.23 m walls, the perpendicular wall's face is 11.5 cm from the endpoint. A window at `offset` 0.10 passes check 4 (10 cm on each side) but its cut box lies inside the adjoining wall's volume. After the union the cut removes that wall's material, leaving a notch. Check 4 should measure to the adjoining wall's face, not the centreline end. The piece-wise fallback ("left piece, lintel above, sill below, right piece") also cannot work once walls have been merged into one polygon, because per-wall identity is gone.

ATTACK 7 [MINOR] Vector Style A rule fails on the common CAD export of one merged wall polygon
Where: Vector path 2, "filled rectangles and polygons wider than 0.08 m are wall pieces. Take each piece's long axis as a centreline and its short side as thickness."
Problem: CAD PDF exports commonly emit the whole wall network as one filled path with holes, or as L- and T-shaped polygons. A single "long axis" is meaningless for such a shape, so one L-shaped or ring-shaped polygon yields one wrong wall. The same rule treats filled furniture, columns, bold text and fixtures wider than 8 cm as walls. The doc has no decomposition step (for example, a skeleton or medial axis of the polygon) for non-rectangular fills.
