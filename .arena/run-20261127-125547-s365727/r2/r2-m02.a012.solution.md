# Turning a 2D floor plan into a 3D model: build plan

## 1. Recommendation in one paragraph

Do not go straight from picture to 3D. Go **picture -> a small, human-checkable JSON description of the plan ("plan IR": walls, openings, footprint, rooms, in metres) -> 3D**. Every 3D output (a three.js viewer, a `.glb` file, later Blender renders) is generated from that one JSON file. That is what makes the walls accurate: you can inspect and fix the JSON, which you cannot do with pixels or with an AI-generated image. Computer vision proposes the geometry; Claude (reading the plan image inside your Claude Code session) supplies what CV is bad at (room names, door versus window, printed dimension text); an overlay image lets you check the result in seconds. Two files keep your work safe: the extractor writes `plan.raw.json` (machine output, regenerate any time) and everything a person or Claude decides lives in `edits.json`, keyed by ids that come from wall positions, so re-running the extractor with a new flag does not lose edits. The minimal first version in section 9 was built and run in this container on synthetic plans (an L-shaped plan with piers, a door near a corner, an open hall gap, solid and double-line walls, a rotated scan, a toy PDF). It has not been run on a real architect's plan, and section 8 lists where I expect it to fail.

## 2. Assumptions (nothing was stated, so I chose these)

1. Input is a **clean architectural plan** (CAD/PDF export or a decent scan), one storey, one image per floor. Scans up to about 10 degrees off axis are straightened by `deskew.py`; photos of paper (perspective) and hand sketches are Phase 3.
2. Walls are **axis-aligned** after deskew. Diagonal and curved walls are Phase 2.
3. The plan has a **known scale**: a printed dimension, a scale bar, or one length you can type in. Lengths may be metric or imperial (`8`, `8.2m`, `8200mm`, `12'-6"`, `41' 3 1/2"`). Without scale there is no true 3D size, so the script refuses to run without `--ppm` or `--dim`.
4. "3D model" means an explorable model plus standard files (`.glb`, later `.blend`) and still renders (top-down, perspective, later eye-level). Not a BIM/IFC model with structure and services (an extension).
5. Defaults when the plan says nothing: ceiling 2.7 m, door height 2.05 m, window sill 0.9 m and height 1.2 m. Defaulted openings carry `"confidence": "low"`.
6. You work in this cloud container (Linux, Python, Node, headless Chromium), with outbound traffic through a proxy that allows only some hosts.
7. "Claude reads the image" means the Claude Code session itself looking at `overlay.png` and `crops/*.png`. No separate API key is assumed. If you later want this unattended, the same prompt can be sent through the Anthropic API; that variant is untested here.

Test results with the code in section 9 (all synthetic; re-run after the latest changes):

- **L-shaped plan** (8 m x 5 m with a 3 m x 2 m notch, walls 0.15 m, 100 px per metre): 8 walls; 3 openings measured at 1.19, 1.19 and 0.89 m (drawn 1.2, 1.2, 0.9); the 0.4 m pier and 0.3 m stub kept; a 1.5 m gap in a partition left open and reported under `gaps`; footprint 36.1 m2 as an L; 1 room. Double-line version: same 3 openings (1.17, 1.17, 0.87 m, about 3 cm narrow), footprint 36.4 m2.
- **Scale check**: `--check` is given a *different* printed dimension than `--dim` (the vertical 5000 mm one); a wrong value gives the 2% warning. A wrong `--ppm` does not reliably say "0 walls found": with `--ppm 30` on the 100 ppm plan the extractor returned 34 junk walls and a 436 m2 footprint, which the `--check` and `--area` options exist to catch.
- **Rotated scan**: the plan rotated by 0.8 degrees and fed straight to `plan2ir.py` gave wall thickness 0.16 to 0.22 m (true 0.15), **2 of 3 openings and a 17 m2 footprint (true 36)**; the rotation warning fired. After `deskew.py` (estimated 0.61 degrees, residual reads 0.00; the estimator is accurate to roughly 0.2 degrees on these tests: 0.5 -> 0.45, 1.5 -> 1.65) the same plan gave thickness 0.16 to 0.17, all 3 openings and 36.2 m2.
- **Stable ids**: re-running with `--max-hole 1.6` (the 1.49 m gap becomes an opening) kept every other wall and opening id; the saved `edits.json` still applied; the new opening was flagged by the count check; the room id changed because the room split in two, and the tool reported it as stale instead of failing.
- **GLB fidelity**: `check_glb.py` found all 8 walls by name in `model.glb` and matched length, height and thickness to `plan.json` within 5 mm.
- **PDF**: a toy vector PDF drawn at 1:100 rasterised with `pdf2png.py`; the printed 118.11 px/m gave a 40.5 m2 footprint for an 8 x 5 m box (40 m2 plus wall thickness). Real CAD PDFs, scans, hatching and furniture are untested.

## 4. Options considered

Scores 1 (poor) to 5 (good). Criteria: **Acc** dimensional accuracy; **Open** doors/windows/semantics; **MVP** speed to a first working version; **Here** works in this container as verified in section 3; **Out** editable, standard output files; **Look** ceiling on visual quality; **Per-plan** effort for each additional plan. Scores are my judgement, not measurements.

| Option | Acc | Open | MVP | Here | Out | Look | Per-plan | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. AI image generator turns the plan into a "3D render" (img2img) | 1 | 1 | 5 | 2 | 1 | 4 | 5 | 19 |
| B. Fully automatic CV -> three.js, no human step | 3 | 2 | 4 | 5 | 4 | 3 | 5 | 26 |
| C. **CV + vision LLM propose a JSON, overlay check, JSON -> three.js/glTF now, Blender later** | 5 | 4 | 3 | 5 | 5 | 4 | 4 | **30** |
| D. Pretrained deep floor-plan segmentation (CubiCasa5K-style), then vectorize | 3 | 3 | 1 | 2 | 4 | 3 | 4 | 20 |
| E. Fully manual tracing in a browser (click corners, type widths) | 5 | 5 | 4 | 5 | 5 | 3 | 2 | 29 |
| F. Same as C but Blender (bpy) as the only backend | 4 | 4 | 2 | 2 | 5 | 5 | 3 | 25 |

Why C: A cannot be trusted for geometry (image generators redraw walls, change sizes and invent furniture; fine as an optional mood picture, never as the model). D needs model weights, ideally a GPU, and a licence check (verify the dataset terms before building on it), and still outputs raster masks. B has no place to catch errors, so a wrong wall ships silently. E is accurate but slow per room, so it is the **fallback editing mode inside C** (`edits.json` is exactly that: add or remove walls and openings by hand). F looks best but is the least runnable here today; because everything hangs off the JSON, Blender becomes a second exporter in Phase 2. C, E and F are close, so the matrix ranks risk rather than measuring it; the tie-breaker is "works in this container today and improves per plan".

## 5. Architecture

```
 plan.pdf / .png / scan
        |
 [0] pdf2png.py (PDF -> PNG, prints implied ppm)  ->  deskew.py (straighten scans)  ->  grid.png: read pixel ends of printed dimensions
        |
 [1] plan2ir.py (OpenCV): remove text -> wall bars -> gaps = openings; flood fill -> footprint + rooms
        |                  writes plan.raw.json + overlay.png + crops/ + warnings   (regenerate freely)
 [2] semantics (Claude reads overlay.png and crops/)  ->  edits.json   (also your manual fixes; survives re-runs)
        |
 [3] apply_edits.py plan.raw.json + edits.json  ->  plan.json   <-- single source of truth for 3D (metres, y down like the image)
        |
   +----+---------------------+-------------------------+
   v                          v                         v
 viewer.html (three.js)   model.glb (GLTFExporter,     Blender bpy (Phase 2)
 + Playwright PNGs        nodes named by wall id)      model.blend, Cycles, walkthrough
                          + check_glb.py
```

### The plan IR (`plan.json`), as written by the MVP
Ids are built from positions in centimetres (`wh121_113` = horizontal wall, centre line at y 1.21 m, starts at x 1.13 m; `wh121_113.118` = the opening whose centre is 1.18 m along it), so the same drawing gives the same ids on every run.

```json
{
  "units": "m", "height": 2.7, "source": "plan.png", "style": "solid",
  "scale": {"method": "dim", "ppm": 100.0, "check_error_pct": 0.0},
  "walls":    [{"id": "wh121_113", "a": [1.13, 1.21], "b": [9.29, 1.21], "t": 0.16, "h": 2.7}],
  "openings": [{"id": "wh121_113.118", "wall": "wh121_113", "type": "window", "offset": 1.18, "width": 1.19,
                "sill": 0.9, "height": 1.2, "confidence": "vision"}],
  "gaps":     [{"from": [5.41, 3.22], "o": "v", "width": 1.49, "hint": "left open (no wall) ..."}],
  "floor":    [[[1.13, 1.13], [1.13, 6.29], [6.29, 6.29], [6.3, 4.29], [9.29, 4.29], [9.29, 1.13]]],
  "rooms":    [{"id": "r474_344", "name": "Living", "area_m2": 31.25, "polygon": [[...]], "floor": "oak", "label_px": [455, 360]}],
  "footprint_area_m2": 36.1,
  "warnings": []
}
```
`a`/`b` are a wall's centreline ends, `t` its thickness, `offset` the distance from `a` along the wall to the opening's centre. Wall coordinates include the image margin (the viewer recentres); subtract the margin if you need origin-at-corner.

## 6. Step-by-step pipeline and how each accuracy problem is handled

**Step 0. Normalise, straighten and calibrate scale (decides all accuracy).**
- **PDF input:** `python pdf2png.py plan.pdf --dpi 300 --scale 100` writes `plan.png` and prints the pixels per metre that a 1:100 drawing implies at that dpi (118.11). Trust a printed dimension over that number: PDFs are often printed "fit to page". It also reports how many vector drawing items the page holds; a CAD export has hundreds, and for those `page.get_drawings()` returns exact line coordinates (Phase 2: it removes pixel error, not the semantic work; I did not write that path).
- **Scans:** run `python deskew.py plan.png plan_deskew.png` and use the output image for everything after. It measures the dominant wall angle with long Hough lines, rotates, and re-measures up to three times. `plan2ir.py` also estimates the angle itself and warns above 0.2 degrees, because skew silently inflates measured thicknesses (0.8 degrees gave 0.22 m for a 0.15 m wall in my test) and drops openings.
- **Reading pixel positions:** run `plan2ir.py plan.png --grid`; it writes `grid.png` with a labelled 100 px grid. Open it, read the pixel x/y of the two ends of a printed dimension (about +/-5 px by eye, so use a long dimension: 5 px on 600 px is under 1%), and pass `--dim x1,y1,x2,y2,LENGTH`. Scale priority: (1) a printed dimension, (2) a scale bar, (3) one typed-in wall length, (4) a standard door leaf of about 0.9 m, as sanity check only.
- **Units:** LENGTH accepts `8`, `8m`, `8000mm`, `800cm`, `12'-6"`, `41' 3 1/2"`, `30"`. Quote imperial values in the shell. Tested: `26'-3"` gave 8.001 m. Use the same unit style as printed on the plan, and let `--check` catch a conversion slip: it only helps if at least one dimension is entered in its own printed units rather than converted by hand.
- **Cross-check:** pass every *other* printed dimension as `--check x1,y1,x2,y2,LENGTH` (repeatable; never the segment used for `--dim`, which would agree with itself by construction). Prefer one in the other direction so a non-uniform scan shows up. The worst error goes to `scale.check_error_pct` and a warning fires above 2%. This proves the drawing scales uniformly; it does not prove the extracted walls match, which is what the overlay is for.

**Step 1. Walls.** Binarise (Otsu). Delete connected blobs smaller than 0.3 m in both directions (letters, ticks, symbols) before anything else. Open with a kernel of the thinnest real wall (`--wall-min`, 0.08 m) to remove lines thinner than a wall, then open with long 1-pixel kernels (`--min-len`, 0.25 m) to keep horizontal and vertical bars. Each bar gives centreline and thickness. `--style hollow` first closes the mask with `--wall-max` (0.35 m) so walls drawn as two parallel lines fill in; `--style auto` (default) tries solid and retries hollow if no walls are found. Piers and corner stubs down to 0.25 m survive.

**Step 2. Openings.** Collinear bars in the same line are grouped. A gap between consecutive bars is classified by width: under 0.4 m = glitch, joined with a warning; 0.4 m to `--max-hole` (1.3 m) = opening, wall merged and a hole recorded; over 1.3 m = **left open** (no wall, no lintel) and listed under `gaps`, because a wide gap in an interior partition is usually a cased opening or open-plan link. Cost of that choice: a genuine 1.8 m window in an outer wall is left open until you re-run with `--max-hole 2.4`. Re-running is now safe (ids are position-based and `edits.json` is separate), which section 3 tested. Opening type cannot be decided from a gap, so every opening starts as `door`, confidence `low`, until step 2b.

**Step 2b. Semantics (Claude reads images).** See section 7.

**Step 3. Footprint and rooms.** Wall rectangles are rasterised from the IR; wide gaps are sealed in a separate mask used only for the outside test. Flood fill from the image border marks the exterior; everything else is the footprint. Its outer contour, simplified at 0.03 m, is the floor slab (`floor`), so L-shapes and notches are correct. Rooms are the connected interior regions of the footprint minus walls (at least 1 m2); each gets an id from its centroid, area and polygon. Limit: an enclosed courtyard cannot be told from a room.

**Step 4. Validate (reported, never fixed).** `plan2ir.py` prints and stores warnings for: image skew over 0.2 degrees; a wall end that touches no perpendicular wall and is not next to a wide gap ("free end"); walls under 0.3 m; openings wider than 85% of their wall; joined cracks; no closed room; worst scale-check error over 2%; footprint area more than 5% away from `--area` (a printed area is often interior and the footprint is measured to outer faces, so expect a gap). `overlay.png` draws walls red, openings blue with ids, wide gaps orange, rooms tinted with ids, on top of the source image.

**Step 5. Build the 3D (geometry rules).**
- Each wall is a rectangle `length x height` in its own vertical plane with one rectangular hole per opening, extruded by the thickness. The mesh is **named with the wall id**, and so is its GLB node.
- Coordinates: plan `(x, y)` -> world `(x, 0, y)`, plan y down maps to +z. The top camera is a true `OrthographicCamera` above the plan centre with `up = (0,0,-1)`, which puts plan y down the screen exactly like the image, so nothing is mirrored (verified: the notch of the L appears at the bottom right in both the plan and the render). Mirroring is the most common silent bug in this kind of project.
- Corners: bars already include the corner squares. For hand-drawn walls, extend each end by `t/2` at T and L joints.
- Floor: one slab per footprint polygon (`slab`), and a thin (3 mm) room polygon on top (`room_<id>`) coloured by the room's `floor` material.
- Door leaves, window glass, ceilings: Phase 2.

**Step 6. Materials.** MVP: plain PBR colours with a floor table (`oak`, `tile`, `concrete`) selected by the room's `floor` field. Map room names to finishes through a small table, never let an AI generator pick per wall, so renders are reproducible. Textures (CC0 sets) in Phase 2, after checking proxy access.

**Step 7. Lighting and cameras.** MVP: hemisphere light plus one shadow-casting directional light aimed at the plan centre, shadow frustum scaled to the plan span (a default frustum of about 10 m clips shadows on a 12 m house). The top view switches shadows off, because wall shadows darkened the floor in my first test render. Phase 2 in Blender: sun and sky, an area light per window placed from the openings list, Cycles with denoising for stills, eye-level cameras at 1.6 m per room.

**Step 8. Export.**

| File | Made by | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `plan.raw.json` | `plan2ir.py` | machine output; regenerate freely |
| `edits.json` | Claude + you | every decision and hand fix, keyed by id; commit it to git |
| `plan.json` | `apply_edits.py` | source of truth for 3D, diffable |
| `overlay.png`, `crops/*.png`, `grid.png` | `plan2ir.py` | verification and the images Claude reads |
| `model.glb` | three.js GLTFExporter via `render.mjs` | opens in Blender, Windows 3D viewer, Quick Look, web; node names = wall ids |
| `render_persp.png`, `render_top.png` | `render.mjs` (Playwright) | client images |
| `viewer.html` | MVP | explorable model; serve over http, not `file://` |
| per-room eye-level renders, `model.blend`, `walkthrough.mp4` | Blender, Phase 2 | not in the MVP |

## 7. Where a vision LLM and where CV are used (concrete protocol)

CV gives coordinates and thicknesses (exact to a pixel). Claude gives meaning. Do not trust an LLM's pixel coordinates; ask for labels, counts and ids only, then validate.

Protocol, run inside the Claude Code session after `plan2ir.py`:
1. Claude opens `overlay.png`, then each `crops/<id>.png` (3 m square around each opening, enlarged to at least 300 px) and the original plan.
2. Prompt used (copy as is): *"Plan image, overlay, and crops attached. Count the door symbols and the window symbols in the whole plan. For each opening id, say door or window (a door shows a swing arc or leaf line; a window shows two or three thin parallel lines across the wall). For each room id, give the name printed in it, if any. Read any printed window sizes such as W1200x1500. Answer only with JSON of this shape: {"counts": {"doors": n, "windows": n}, "openings": {"wh121_113.118": {"type": "window", "sill": 0.9, "height": 1.2}}, "rooms": {"r474_344": {"name": "Living", "floor": "oak"}}}. If unsure about an item, omit it."*
3. Save the answer as `edits.json` (merge into the existing file if there is one) and run `python apply_edits.py plan.raw.json edits.json`.
4. **What the count check does.** Labels for ids that exist are always applied; the check never blocks a correct label. It compares the counted doors+windows `n` with the extractor: if `n` is more than openings + wide gaps, the output says how many are missing and tells you to add them (`add_openings`: wall id, offset, width, type) or re-run with a larger `--max-hole`; if `n` is less than the number of openings, it says how many are false positives and tells you to list them under `remove_openings`. These notes go into `plan.json` `warnings`, so a stale plan is visible. The only hard refusal is self-contradiction: more openings typed `door` (or `window`) than the counts say, in which case types are skipped unless `--force`. Unknown ids are reported as stale and skipped. Tested: a counts mismatch applied the label and printed "1 missing -> add_openings"; an add plus a remove brought the counts into range with no note.
5. What is not validated: that each individual door/window label is right. The crops make that a ten-second visual check per opening. I did not measure how accurate Claude is at this on real plans. If it is poor, the fallback is the manual edit of `type`, and nothing else in the pipeline changes.

## 8. Risks and failure cases

| Failure case | What goes wrong | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| No or wrong scale | Everything wrong-sized but plausible | Script refuses without `--ppm`/`--dim`; independent `--check` over 2% warns; a wrong ppm can give junk walls (34 walls, 436 m2 in my test) rather than an error, so use `--check` and `--area` |
| Imperial/metric slip | Plausible wrong size | Enter lengths as printed (`12'-6"`), check with a second dimension in the other axis |
| Scan skew | Walls mis-measured, openings dropped | `deskew.py`; the extractor warns above 0.2 degrees; accuracy of the angle about 0.2 degrees on tests |
| Photo perspective | Non-uniform scale | 4-point homography; Phase 3 |
| Double-line (hollow) walls | Nothing found in solid mode | `--style hollow` (tested on synthetic); window symbols drawn inside a gap fill the gap in hollow mode, so those windows are missed: add them with `add_openings` |
| Hatched or poche walls | Hatch lines thinner than `--wall-min` vanish | try `--style hollow`, or fill the poche in the PDF; untested on real hatch |
| Heavy furniture or fixtures drawn thick | False walls | overlay review, list under `remove_walls`; free-end warnings help |
| Thin partitions below `--wall-min` | Walls missing | lower `--wall-min`; overlay shows the miss |
| Window piers, corner stubs | Dropped, causing missed openings | kept down to 0.25 m (tested); anything under 0.25 m is dropped silently apart from a free-end warning |
| Wide gap in an exterior wall (large window) | Left open instead of a window | listed under `gaps`; re-run with `--max-hole` (safe: ids are stable); footprint is unaffected |
| Hall opening 1.0 to 1.3 m between partition stubs | Becomes a door hole with lintel | review the crops, `remove_openings` or edit its `height` |
| Door drawn open with no gap | Missing door | count check reports a shortfall; add with `add_openings` |
| Re-running the extractor | Used to wipe edits | `plan.raw.json` is the only file it writes; edits live in `edits.json` (tested) |
| Diagonal or curved walls | Dropped | out of scope; Phase 2 |
| Stairs, columns, balconies, sloped ceilings, double height | Not representable | unsupported; placeholders Phase 3 |
| Several floors on one sheet | Merged garbage | crop manually first |
| Courtyard enclosed by walls | Floored as if inside | edit `floor` polygon |
| Browser cannot load libraries (CDNs blocked here) | Blank page | local `node_modules` (done); `file://` fetch also blocked, so use the http server in `render.mjs` |
| Playwright wants a different Chromium than installed | `launch()` fails | `CHROMIUM_PATH` as in section 9 |
| Software GL slow and noisy | Slow shadows | small scenes; Cycles later |
| Confidential plans sent to external services | Leak | keep processing local; external image APIs only with permission |
| AI picture shown as if it were the model | Misleading sizes | label "illustration, not to scale" |

## 9. The minimal first version (runnable now)

Time to build: about half a day with your own plan. Folder `plan3d/`. Put the files below in it (`gen_test.py` is optional: it makes the test plan I used; `pdf2png.py` is needed only for PDFs, `deskew.py` is imported by `plan2ir.py` so keep it in the same folder).

```bash
mkdir plan3d && cd plan3d
python3 -m venv v && ./v/bin/pip install opencv-python-headless numpy pillow pymupdf
npm init -y && npm i three@0.160.0 playwright          # then use the installed Chromium, see below
python3 gen_test.py                                    # optional: writes plan.png (test L-plan; args: [solid|hollow] [rotation_deg])
# PDF input: ./v/bin/python pdf2png.py plan.pdf --scale 100      # scans: ./v/bin/python deskew.py plan.png plan_deskew.png, then use that file below
./v/bin/python plan2ir.py plan.png --grid              # 1. writes grid.png; read the pixel ends of the printed dimensions from it
./v/bin/python plan2ir.py plan.png --dim 120,40,920,40,8.0 --check 40,120,40,620,5.0   # 2. writes plan.raw.json, overlay.png, crops/ (the --check is the OTHER, vertical dimension)
# 3. open overlay.png and read the warnings; in the Claude Code session, run the section 7 protocol -> edits.json
./v/bin/python apply_edits.py plan.raw.json edits.json  # -> plan.json (re-run steps 2-3 as often as you like; edits.json is kept)
CHROMIUM_PATH=/opt/pw-browsers/chromium-1243/chrome-linux64/chrome node render.mjs   # writes render_persp.png, render_top.png, model.glb
./v/bin/python check_glb.py model.glb plan.json        # every wall in the GLB, by id, against plan.json
```
(In another container, if `npx playwright install chromium` works, omit `CHROMIUM_PATH`. `render.mjs` and `viewer.html` read `plan.json`.) With your own plan use `--ppm N` instead of `--dim` if you already know pixels per metre.

Flags you will touch: `--style auto|solid|hollow`, `--max-hole` (default 1.3 m), `--min-len` (0.25 m), `--wall-min` (0.08 m), `--wall-max` (0.35 m), `--area`, `--height`, `--out`.

### `plan2ir.py` (tested on the synthetic plans)

```python
#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""plan2ir.py  plan.png (--ppm N | --dim x1,y1,x2,y2,LENGTH) [options]
LENGTH is metres by default; also 3.81m, 3810mm, 381cm, 12'-6", 41' 3 1/2" (quote it in the shell).
Axis-aligned walls, openings, footprint and rooms from a raster floor plan.
Writes plan.raw.json (metres, y down like the image; machine output, safe to regenerate; hand edits live in edits.json), overlay.png, grid.png, crops/<opening>.png.
Never auto-fixes: everything doubtful goes into plan.json "warnings" and "gaps"."""
import argparse, json, math, os, sys
import re
import cv2, numpy as np
sys.path.insert(0, os.path.dirname(os.path.abspath(__file__)))
from deskew import skew_angle

ap = argparse.ArgumentParser()
ap.add_argument("image")
ap.add_argument("--ppm", type=float, help="pixels per metre")
ap.add_argument("--dim", help="x1,y1,x2,y2,LENGTH : two pixel points on a printed dimension -> ppm")
ap.add_argument("--check", action="append", default=[], help="x1,y1,x2,y2,LENGTH : another, DIFFERENT printed dimension; repeatable")
ap.add_argument("--area", type=float, help="printed total area (m2) to compare with the footprint")
ap.add_argument("--style", choices=["auto", "solid", "hollow"], default="auto",
                help="solid = filled black walls; hollow = walls drawn as two thin lines")
ap.add_argument("--wall-min", type=float, default=0.08, help="thinnest real wall (m)")
ap.add_argument("--wall-max", type=float, default=0.35, help="thickest real wall (m); closing size for hollow style")
ap.add_argument("--min-len", type=float, default=0.25, help="shortest wall piece kept (m); piers and corner stubs need this small")
ap.add_argument("--min-gap", type=float, default=0.40, help="narrowest opening (m)")
ap.add_argument("--max-hole", type=float, default=1.30,
                help="widest gap turned into a wall hole (m). Wider gaps stay open and are listed under 'gaps'")
ap.add_argument("--height", type=float, default=2.7)

def parse_len(s):
    """'8' or '8m' -> metres; '8000mm'; '800cm'; feet-inches: 12'-6", 41' 3 1/2", 6', 30" ; bare number = metres"""
    s = s.strip().lower().replace("\u2019", "'").replace("\u201d", '"').replace("''", '"')
    for suf, f in (("mm", 1e-3), ("cm", 1e-2), ("m", 1.0)):
        if s.endswith(suf) and re.fullmatch(r"[\d.]+", s[:-len(suf)].strip()): return float(s[:-len(suf)]) * f
    if re.fullmatch(r"[\d.]+", s): return float(s)
    mt = re.fullmatch(r"(?:(\d+(?:\.\d+)?)\s*')?[\s-]*(?:(\d+)?[\s-]*(?:(\d+)\s*/\s*(\d+))?\s*\")?", s)
    if not mt or not s: sys.exit(f"cannot read length {s!r}")
    ft, inch, num, den = mt.groups()
    return ((float(ft or 0) * 12) + float(inch or 0) + (int(num) / int(den) if num else 0)) * 0.0254
```

### `deskew.py` (tested at 0.5, 0.8 and 1.5 degrees)

```python
#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""deskew.py plan.png [out.png]   Straightens a slightly rotated scan so walls are axis-aligned.
Estimates the dominant wall angle from long Hough lines (folded to -45..45 degrees, weighted by length),
rotates about the centre on a white canvas (rotation keeps the scale, so ppm is unchanged).
Use the OUTPUT image for --grid/--dim/--check, because pixel positions move."""
import sys, math
import cv2, numpy as np

def skew_angle(g):
    """degrees to rotate the image by (counter-clockwise positive in cv2.getRotationMatrix2D) to straighten it; 0.0 if unsure"""
    _, ink = cv2.threshold(g, 0, 255, cv2.THRESH_BINARY_INV | cv2.THRESH_OTSU)
    H, W = g.shape
    ls = cv2.HoughLinesP(ink, 1, math.pi / 1800, threshold=80, minLineLength=int(0.15 * min(H, W)), maxLineGap=6)
    if ls is None: return 0.0
    ang, wt = [], []
    for x1, y1, x2, y2 in ls.reshape(-1, 4):
        a = math.degrees(math.atan2(y2 - y1, x2 - x1)) % 90.0
        if a > 45: a -= 90
        if abs(a) < 10: ang.append(a); wt.append(math.hypot(x2 - x1, y2 - y1))
    if len(ang) < 4: return 0.0
    o = np.argsort(ang); ang, wt = np.array(ang)[o], np.array(wt)[o]
    return float(ang[np.searchsorted(np.cumsum(wt), wt.sum() / 2)])     # weighted median

if __name__ == "__main__":
    src = sys.argv[1]; dst = sys.argv[2] if len(sys.argv) > 2 else "plan_deskew.png"
    g = cv2.imread(src, cv2.IMREAD_GRAYSCALE)
    if g is None: sys.exit("cannot read image")
    total = 0.0
    for _ in range(3):                                   # re-estimate after each rotation: a single Hough pass is only good to ~0.2 deg
        a = skew_angle(g)
        if abs(a) < 0.05: break
        H, W = g.shape; M = cv2.getRotationMatrix2D((W / 2, H / 2), a, 1.0)
        c, s = abs(M[0, 0]), abs(M[0, 1]); nW, nH = int(H * s + W * c), int(H * c + W * s)
        M[0, 2] += nW / 2 - W / 2; M[1, 2] += nH / 2 - H / 2
        g = cv2.warpAffine(g, M, (nW, nH), flags=cv2.INTER_LINEAR, borderValue=255); total += a
    print(f"total correction {total:+.2f} deg; residual estimate {skew_angle(g):+.2f} deg")
    cv2.imwrite(dst, g); print(f"wrote {dst} ({g.shape[1]}x{g.shape[0]}); use it for every later step, including --grid/--dim/--check")
```

```python
"""apply_edits.py plan.raw.json edits.json [--out plan.json] [--force]
plan.raw.json = machine output of plan2ir.py (regenerate freely). edits.json = everything a human or Claude decided; it is keyed by the
position-based ids, so it survives re-runs. Output plan.json = raw + edits. Stale ids are reported, never fatal.
edits.json:
 {"counts":          {"doors": 2, "windows": 3},                       # what the reader counted in the whole image (optional)
```

### `check_glb.py` (tested)

```python
#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""check_glb.py model.glb plan.json  -> compares every named wall node in the GLB with plan.json (no Blender needed).
A wall mesh is built as length x height x thickness before rotation, so its local POSITION bounds give those three numbers."""
import json, struct, sys
b = open(sys.argv[1], "rb").read(); plan = json.load(open(sys.argv[2]))
assert b[:4] == b"glTF", "not a GLB"
n = struct.unpack("<I", b[12:16])[0]; j = json.loads(b[20:20 + n])
bad = 0; seen = 0
walls = {w["id"]: w for w in plan["walls"]}
for nd in j["nodes"]:
    if nd.get("name") in walls and "mesh" in nd:
        w = walls[nd["name"]]; seen += 1
        acc = j["accessors"][j["meshes"][nd["mesh"]]["primitives"][0]["attributes"]["POSITION"]]
        dx, dy, dz = [acc["max"][i] - acc["min"][i] for i in range(3)]
        L = ((w["b"][0] - w["a"][0]) ** 2 + (w["b"][1] - w["a"][1]) ** 2) ** .5
        ok = abs(dx - L) < .005 and abs(dz - w["t"]) < .005 and abs(dy - w["h"]) < .005
        bad += not ok; print(f"{w['id']:14s} glb {dx:.3f} x {dy:.3f} x {dz:.3f}  plan {L:.3f} x {w['h']:.3f} x {w['t']:.3f}  {'ok' if ok else 'MISMATCH'}")
print(f"{seen} of {len(walls)} walls found by name in the GLB; {bad} mismatched"); sys.exit(1 if bad or seen != len(walls) else 0)
```

### Known limits of this MVP code

- Opening type is a guess until the semantics step or a manual edit; `door` is the default.
- Axis-aligned walls only (after deskew); pieces under 0.25 m are dropped. Deskew handles rotation, not perspective.
- Gaps wider than `--max-hole` are never holes (see step 2).
- Ids come from positions in centimetres. If a re-run shifts a wall by one pixel across a centimetre boundary the id changes and that edit is reported as stale (not applied silently); fix the id in `edits.json`.
- Measured gap widths are about 1 to 3 cm narrower on double-line plans, and anti-aliased edges add a similar error on solid plans; at 100 ppm one pixel is 1 cm, at 30 ppm 3 cm, so scan at 200 dpi or more.
- Windows are plain holes (no glass), doors have no leaves, there is no ceiling.
- The GLB exports without the hemisphere light (the exporter warns); geometry, names and materials export. Room polygons sit 3 mm above the slab, which can show z-fighting at grazing angles in some viewers.
- `--check` and `--area` validate the drawing and the footprint; they cannot find a wall the extractor never saw (the overlay is for that). `check_glb.py` proves the GLB equals `plan.json`, not that `plan.json` equals the drawing.

## 10. Roadmap after the MVP

1. **Real-plan hardening (first):** run on 3 to 5 of your own plans, record every miss in the overlay, and tune the flags; add vector-PDF extraction if your plans are CAD exports.
2. **Rooms and finishes:** per-room material table from names, door leaves, window glass, ceilings, cutaway toggle.
3. **Blender backend:** `pip install bpy` (test first), a script that reads `plan.json`, builds walls with `bmesh`, cuts openings with Boolean difference of box cutters, adds frames, sun and area lights, Cycles stills, `.blend`/`.glb`. Alternatively drive a running Blender through its MCP add-on if you have Blender on your own machine.
4. **Editor:** a canvas page to drag wall ends and toggle opening types, writing back to `edits.json`.
5. **Hard inputs:** diagonal walls, hand sketches, photos with homography, multi-storey, optional IFC export.

## 11. Acceptance test (how you know it works)

Use one real plan with at least three printed dimensions (one for `--dim`, at least two other, in both directions, for `--check`). Each check maps to a file the MVP produces:
(a) `plan.json` `scale.check_error_pct` is at most 2, from `--check` on dimensions **other than** the one used for `--dim`.
(b) Doors and windows: the counts in `edits.json` equal what you count by eye on the plan, and `apply_edits.py` prints no "missing" or "false positive" note.
(c) `overlay.png`: every wall in the source image has a red outline, no red outline sits on empty paper, and `warnings` in `plan.json` is empty or each entry is explained (including no skew warning).
(d) `render_top.png` has the same orientation as the original plan (compare a distinctive corner; not mirrored) and the same outline (an L stays an L).
(e) Size: `python check_glb.py model.glb plan.json` prints `N of N walls found by name ... 0 mismatched` (length, height and thickness within 5 mm). In Blender the same nodes appear under their wall ids.
Pass only if all five hold.
