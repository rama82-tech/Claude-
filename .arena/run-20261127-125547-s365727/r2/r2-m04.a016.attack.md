ATTACK 1 [MAJOR] The 3.5 m gap-bridging rule silently defeats the "fail loudly" and exterior-closed gates
Where: Stage 4 step 2 "If `g <= 3.5 m`, **bridge it** ... the gap becomes an opening record"; step 3 "Wide gap with no door symbol: `arch`"; gate "the exterior must form a closed ring after bridging"; recommendation paragraph "if ... the exterior does not close ... the pipeline stops".
Problem: Any collinear gap up to 3.5 m is converted into an opening, whatever caused it. A 2.5 m stretch of exterior wall that CV missed (faded scan, hatch misread) becomes a 2.5 m full-height "arch" in the exterior wall, the ring "closes", the gate passes, and the only trace is a lowered confidence value. Two unrelated collinear walls (the end walls of two rooms either side of a 3 m corridor or courtyard) are likewise merged into one run. The only failure test in "Success tests" uses a gap larger than 3.5 m, so the silent case is never tested. The design's central promise (no plausible-but-wrong house) fails exactly for the most common CV error, a missed wall segment.

ATTACK 2 [MAJOR] Opening `offset` origin and the "opening inside its run" check use different origins
Where: "Openings belong to a run and are positioned by `offset` from the run's start along the centreline" versus severity table "`offset + width <= run length`, where run length is measured between the inner faces of the perpendicular end walls".
Problem: Offset is measured from the centreline start, but the bound is the inner-face length, so the inequality mixes two origins. Example from the plan.json: w1 runs (0,0) to (4.2,0), thickness 0.2, inner-face span 0.1 to 4.1 (length 4.0). A valid 0.9 m door at offset 3.15 has its far edge at centreline position 4.05, which is inside the inner face at 4.1. The check computes 3.15 + 0.9 = 4.05 > 4.0 and raises an error, so a correct plan is blocked. A door at offset 0.05 sits inside the corner wall footprint, yet 0.05 >= 0 passes. The user cannot implement this check as written without choosing an origin for the offset.

ATTACK 3 [MAJOR] Window symbols are discarded in stage 3, so stage 4 cannot detect them as described
Where: Stage 3 "Separate thick wall strokes from thin dimension, furniture and text lines by stroke width ... This stage does not close door gaps"; Stage 4 step 3 "Windows: a place where the wall run is continuous but the drawing shows thin parallel lines or a thinner stroke inside it".
Problem: The thin parallel lines of a window are exactly what stage 3 filters out as thin strokes, so stage 4 never sees them. The common drawing convention is a break in the thick wall with thin glazing lines across the break. That looks like a gap, so it takes the "gap" branch. A 1.5 m window gap with no swing arc is then classified as an "arch" (sill 0, full height) or, at 0.7 to 1.2 m, a door. The stage 4 window rule only fits windows drawn as a thinner stroke inside a continuous wall. No pipeline step looks for thin lines inside a gap, so windows in the typical style become doorways or arches.

ATTACK 4 [MAJOR] The v1 front end contradicts the plan's own accuracy claim
Where: Accuracy section "Geometry comes from measurement ... never from an AI model's guess"; Minimal first version step 2 (Claude vision reports wall endpoints "in pixel coordinates").
Problem: In v1 all wall coordinates and openings come from a vision model's estimate. The CV snap only aligns to detected line ends, and CV wall detection is deferred to step 6(b). The grid is drawn "every 100 px" on an image that may be 300 dpi (a 3300 px page for A4 landscape), and vision models downscale large images, so 100 px grid cells would be unreadable or resampled. Nothing says to downscale or tile the image to the model's input size. As written, the user cannot tell whether v1 produces measured or guessed geometry, and the guessed-geometry case is what the plan says never happens.

ATTACK 5 [MAJOR] A single global `dim_reference` cannot represent real dimensioned plans
Where: Assumption 4 and Geometry conventions "`dim_reference` (face by default)", plus plan.json field `"dim_reference": "face"`.
Problem: Dimensioned plans normally mix interior room dimensions (face to face) with exterior overall or chain dimensions (outside face to outside face, or to gridlines at centrelines). One plan-wide setting makes every dimension of another kind compare wrongly. With 0.2 m walls, an overall exterior dimension of 8.4 m compared against a face-to-face measurement gives about 0.4 m (about 5 percent) of false disagreement. This feeds the scale rule (b), where "disagree by more than 5 percent" becomes "scale ambiguous" and stops the pipeline. Each dimension needs its own reference type recorded in `evidence`, but the schema's evidence entries (`label`, `px`, `ok`) have no such field.

ATTACK 6 [MAJOR] The tolerances are inconsistent: 5 percent is too loose for the stated accuracy goal
Where: Stage 2(b) "agree within 5 percent"; Success tests "within 2 percent"; Geometry gate "within 5 mm".
Problem: Accepting a scale whose evidence disagrees by 5 percent means a 10 m building can be off by up to 0.5 m, yet the success test demands 2 percent and the build gate demands 5 mm. The 5 mm gate compares the model with bounds computed from the same plan.json, so it checks only the builder, never fidelity to the drawing. It cannot catch a wrong scale, a missed wall or a wrong thickness. The plan has no test that compares model output against an independent measurement of the source, for example a printed overall dimension, other than the 2 percent check that exists only when two printed face-to-face dimensions are present.

ATTACK 7 [MINOR] Room floors built from `polygon_net` leave holes at door thresholds and under walls
Where: Stage 4 step 5 (shrink each cell by half-thickness to get `polygon_net`) and Stage 7 "Floors: one mesh per room from `polygon_net`, thickness 0.1 m".
Problem: The net polygons stop at the wall faces, and the walls are bridged across doors, so the strip under every doorway (wall thickness wide, door width long) has no floor slab. Walls start at floor level, so eye-level and isometric renders show a gap or void at each door sill, and the exterior has no slab under the walls. No stage adds a threshold or a footprint slab. This contradicts "accurate walls, openings, floors" in the task.
