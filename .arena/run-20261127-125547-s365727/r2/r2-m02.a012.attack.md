ATTACK 1 [MAJOR] GLB axis convention never handled, so every render is sideways
Where: "Step 7: extrude each band with trimesh.creation.extrude_polygon(footprint, z1 - z0) and translate by z0" with "Top-down: OrthographicCamera placed above the bounding-box centre looking down. Iso: camera at the direction (1, 1, 1)". Also "Eye-level camera height 1.5 m".
Problem: extrude_polygon builds the building with Z as up, and trimesh exports the GLB unrotated. glTF and three.js are Y-up. Loaded as is, the house lies on its side: the plan plane is XY (a vertical wall in the viewer) and the "above" camera looks at the elevation. The plan never states a Z-up to Y-up rotation (or a Blender Z-up import setting), so the "done when" checks for topdown.png and iso.png fail on the first run. The user would have to find this by guessing.

ATTACK 2 [MAJOR] Door gaps are handled in two incompatible ways, so openings can be attached to a wall that does not exist at the opening
Where: plan.json schema (walls are single centre-lines, openings carry "wall" and "at") versus Step 3 (skeleton, then "merge collinear pieces") and Step 5 ("Doors are gaps... add a bridging rectangle (opening width by wall thickness) to the union").
Problem: Step 5 assumes the extracted walls have gaps at doors (otherwise there would be nothing to bridge). But the schema locates an opening by "wall" id plus the distance "at" along that wall. If extraction leaves a gap, the door lies between two wall segments, and "at" 1.2 on wall 0 points to a spot with no wall. The band cut then removes nothing and the door never appears as a hole. If extraction instead merges collinear pieces across the gap, there is no gap and the bridging step is dead. The plan does not say which one the extractor produces, so the user cannot know which wall index to type into plan.json from overlay.png. This is the core v1 manual step.

ATTACK 3 [MAJOR] Scale and --ref-face are specified incoherently
Where: Step 2 route 2: `--ref "x1,y1,x2,y2=12'-6\"" --ref-face outer`, and "The default is outer".
Problem: Scale is real length divided by pixel distance between the two points given. The face convention only matters if the points are placed on specific wall faces, but the points come from a labelled 100 px grid, read by eye (by Claude or the user). The plan never says how --ref-face changes the computation: whether it corrects the pixel distance by a wall thickness that is not yet measured, or tells the user where to put the points. A 100 px grid read by eye gives roughly 10 px error. On a 1200 px dimension that is about 1 percent, which uses up half the 2 percent budget before any wall-face error. The user cannot implement the flag as described without guessing.

ATTACK 4 [MAJOR] The acceptance test for accuracy is circular
Where: "Automated check: the outer-face bounding box of the wall union must match the printed overall width and length within 2 percent" and "Done when... the outer-face bounding box matches the printed overall size within 2 percent".
Problem: In route 2 the scale is set from a printed dimension with --ref-face outer, usually the overall width. Checking the outer box against that same dimension passes by construction. A wrong wall extraction, such as a merged or missed wall or a wrong thickness on one side, still passes whenever the reference points are consistent with the extracted outline. The only independent check is "two or three other printed dimensions within 3 percent" (Step 2), and that uses a different threshold from the 2 percent "done" criterion. The plan does not say which one decides success. The 2 percent claim about accuracy is not actually tested.

ATTACK 5 [MAJOR] Double-line extraction will turn window and door symbols into fake walls
Where: "pair parallel segments whose perpendicular distance is within the plausible thickness range (0.08 to 0.35 m)... Unpaired segments (dimension lines, furniture) are dropped."
Problem: Window symbols in the usual CAD style are 2 to 3 parallel thin lines spanning the wall gap, and door jambs and sill lines are parallel pairs too. Windows with spacing of 0.08 m or more pair up and pass the filter. Parallel furniture edges (a 0.1 to 0.3 m deep bookshelf, a counter edge, a stair tread pair) also fall inside the range. The "unpaired are dropped" rule does not catch these. Mitigation is only "overlay review", so wall misdetection is pushed onto the user for every symbol-rich plan. Also, PyMuPDF `get_drawings()` returns rectangles ("re" and "qu" items) and polylines, not only line segments. Wall poche is often a filled "re". The plan only says "line segments" and never says how rectangles are decomposed.

ATTACK 6 [MINOR] Auto-fallback threshold is garbled and cannot be implemented
Where: "if total wall length is under a sanity threshold (for example under 4 times the page diagonal in metres divided by 10)".
Problem: Read literally this is 0.4 times the page diagonal. A page diagonal measured in metres is paper size, not building size. The plan's own scale step gives a real-world size only after scale is known, and a poche plan with text can easily exceed 0.4 times the diagonal in noise strokes alone. A plan of 10 m by 8 m on A3 paper at 1:100 has a drawn wall length well above the threshold even when extraction is mostly text strokes, so the fallback never fires and the model is silently wrong. The "never silently emits an empty model" claim covers only the zero-wall case.

ATTACK 7 [MINOR] Cutaway and top-down steps are underspecified
Where: "house_cutaway.glb is the same build with every band clipped at 1.2 m" and "Top-down: OrthographicCamera placed above... looking down".
Problem: 1.2 m is not one of the z breakpoints. A window with sill 0.9 and h 1.2 spans 0.9 to 2.1 m. A band-by-band build has no band edge at 1.2, so clipping mid-band needs an extra breakpoint or a truncated extrude. The plan does not say which. The top-down camera looks along -Y, parallel to the default up vector (0,1,0), so camera.up must be set explicitly or the orientation is arbitrary. The plan never says this, so the top-down render can come out rotated differently from plan.json and from the overlay.
